## Interpretation and unresolved data issues

- This is one participant ID, one recording day, two tasks and two processing versions, not four independent sessions. Population participant counts, cohort balance, training trial counts, missingness, emotion distributions, annotation agreement and outlier rates are **not estimable from the supplied files**. Missing CSVs are not empty datasets. `TESTSUBJECT` must become missing, never a numeric value or emotion category; retain those rows for coverage accounting and report labeled subsets separately.
- Obtain **`labels_train.csv` and `participants_train.csv` from the authorized training release**, placing them directly under `data/` (or pass `--data`). These two small tables suffice for the population annotation EDA. The repository already supplies `starting_notebooks/markers.csv`; obtain the release-root `markers.csv` too to confirm version correspondence. No full dataset download is needed.
- For supervised sample experiments obtain **P001 day-1 emotion-video BDF and matching record_map**, preferably in both versions. Both metadata files describe that segment, but neither signal file is supplied. Existing video event annotations do not establish the final training label or a verified join. Do not treat `emotion_label` as `final_emotion_label`, or `valence_score` as `rating_valence` without provenance checks. Metadata includes annotations for tasks not exported locally.
- A subject-identity linkage across cohort IDs is needed to guarantee person-disjoint evaluation; participant ID alone may not identify a unique person. Session and stimulus dependencies also require explicit grouped splits.
- **Gaze and pupillometry are excluded from every signal read, feature, fusion and proposed experiment because of the organizer's timing warning supplied in the task.** We have not independently located the original warning. Header/record-map inspection is not validation of eye-stream timing. In CURATED resting-state, the map nominal rate is 200 Hz, effective rate about 166.358 Hz and BDF rate 166 Hz. Do not infer reliable alignment from the paper's upsampling description or the example loader's reconstruction.
- Clip signal reads to `min(record_map.TotalSamples, segment.n_samples, channel stored samples)`. BDF files pad to whole storage records; padding is not physiological missingness. The metadata duration is the first-to-last sample interval, so `n/fs` exceeds it by one sample. Canonical event timing must be converted with `event.onset_s - metadata_time_origin_s`, then checked for overlap and partial clipping. `segment_events.csv` recomputes these overlaps; the supplied `event_indices` lists are empty despite overlapping events.
- PREPROCESSED artefact is shorter than CURATED; comparisons must use the common available interval after checking source origins, not row position or the requested end time. Channel indices also change after T9/T10 removal. CURATED artefact already ends before its requested boundary, and PREPROCESSED is shorter again.
- For artefact, CURATED source origin is 1974.273 s and PREPROCESSED source origin is 1825.642 s, while both metadata origins are 1974.273 s: source cropping changes the source timeline. Canonical metadata alignment, not equal source times, provides the candidate common interval. One metadata video event starts at segment-relative 848.620 s and lasts 10 s, extending 1.813 s beyond the described emotion-video segment; it is flagged partial, and that signal is absent in both versions.
- ECG has a large negative offset and implausibly large values in the declared **mV** units. Header and record-map unit agreement does not prove physical scaling. Treat ECG amplitude as unresolved; ask organizers for the ECG gain/offset and export-unit confirmation before using amplitude features. Do not silently divide by 1,000. Respiration AU cannot be interpreted as calibrated volume. GSR conversion is nonlinear and endpoint-clamped; the script reports out-of-calibration fractions before conversion.
- No NaNs or digital rails does not establish good signal quality. `signal_quality.csv` reports nonfinite samples, digital rail hits, repeated adjacent digital codes, SD, percentiles, 49-51 Hz power fraction, GSR calibration clipping and broad amplitude review indicators. The 100 uV EEG and 100 mV ECG indicators are review heuristics, not clinical thresholds or automatic rejection criteria; EEG DC offsets confound absolute-amplitude counts. PSD uses detrended Welch windows of 4 seconds. Traces use display subsampling, while all metrics use every valid sample. No heart-rate, HRV or breathing-rate estimate is asserted without beat/cycle validation.
- Sparse digital endpoint hits occur in CURATED EEG; export quantization/scaling can also produce these, so they do not prove acquisition saturation. CURATED artefact Fz and FC1 have especially large SD (about 6,981 and 6,406 uV). PREPROCESSED residual artefact amplitudes remain substantial; lower SD and suppressed 50 Hz power alone do not prove that neural information is preserved. GSR shows no samples outside the supplied calibration interval in any of the four recordings.
- The supplied combined `flagged_outliers` indicator is not a ground-truth error label. Report flags including missing values, stratify by cohort/emotion and compare analyses retaining versus excluding flagged rows. Repeated ratings may reflect real experiences. Do not reconstruct the organizer's final labels or outlier algorithm from incomplete intermediate fields.

## Dataset-paper claims (not established by this sample)

Source: repository `docs/Dataset_Paper.pdf`, physical PDF pages cited below; release-specific definitions: `DATASET.md`.

- Paper pp. 1-3: 383 unique participants, 690 sessions, 345 recording hours, 252 video stimuli; five cohorts; 64 EEG channels and ECG/skin conductance/respiration at 1,000 Hz. Dominance is available in cohorts 3-5; rating scales and presentation order vary across cohorts. Multiple IDs can belong to one person.
- Paper pp. 4-5: T9/T10 excluded, EEG filtered 0.5-48 Hz, average referenced and cleaned by Picard/ICLabel; peripheral signals receive a 50 Hz notch. Here 64 versus 62 EEG channels is directly checked; the rest of the preprocessing procedure is not independently reproduced or proven by lower variance.
- Paper pp. 7, 11-14, 18: final emotion labels combine default, expected, ranked self-report and valence/arousal KDE information. Repetition and Mahalanobis criteria contribute to combined flags. The supplement has a threshold-description inconsistency: p. 11 describes proportions exceeding mean + 1.5 SD, whereas p. 18 lists absolute z > 1.5. Clarify before attempting exact reconstruction. Intermediate KDE scores are absent from release columns.
- `DATASET.md` describes 29 held-out participants and gives an example of 27,248 labeled trials from 317 participant IDs. These are **release-document claims**, not observed counts here; the paper and hackathon release can differ. Its held-out wording is also ambiguous: it says every field is `TESTSUBJECT` but then says identifiers remain visible. The EDA retains raw sentinel counts per field and checks actual identifiers when CSVs arrive.
- Annotation-source agreement is descriptive consistency. Derived labels are dependent on their inputs, so pairwise agreement or Cohen's kappa is not independent inter-rater reliability or proof of subjective truth.

## Three research questions and required experiments (hypotheses)

### 1. Can brain-body disagreement predict personalized emotional responses beyond stimulus identity?

Hypothesis: EEG-autonomic complementarity predicts first-ranked subjective emotion and valence/arousal particularly when expected and default emotion disagree. This tests individualized responses rather than recovery of a rule-derived final label.

Required fields: participant identity linkage, cohort, day, video, start/end markers, expected/default labels, expected_emotion_source, self_reported_emotion_1..3, rating_valence/arousal, final label and flags for sensitivity analysis; event times/origins; EEG, ECG (after scale clarification), GSR and respiration with record maps. Population coverage and adequate discordant cases must first be established from the CSVs.

Experiment: fit simple EEG bandpower and peripheral rate/variability/tonic-phasic feature models, then regularized late fusion; compare each modality and a stimulus/expected-label baseline. Evaluate held-out participants and held-out videos separately, plus jointly disjoint splits where feasible; stratify concordant/discordant expected/default trials. Use nested group validation, train-only feature scaling and tuning, macro-F1 for emotion and MAE for ratings. Report participant-bootstrap intervals and flag/coverage sensitivity. Ratings and label-source fields define targets/strata and must not leak into physiology predictors. A fusion gain only on shared videos would weaken the hypothesis.

### 2. Can artefact calibration teach a multimodal model when to abstain across sessions?

Hypothesis: participant-specific artefact signatures identify unreliable windows and improve risk-versus-coverage of emotion predictions more than generic amplitude rejection does.

Required fields: artefact instruction_text/marker/onset/duration, resting and emotion-video signals, segment origins, paired processing versions, participant/day/cohort and available subjective trial targets. Use EEG, ECG, GSR and respiration only; no gaze/pupil. Resolve artefact truncation and ECG scaling first.

Experiment: derive signal-quality features on instructed versus quiet-rest windows, then test transfer to a different day and held-out participants. Compare no gating, fixed quality thresholds and train-only learned gating with the same downstream model, reporting selective MAE/F1, calibration and coverage. Compare CURATED and PREPROCESSED over common intervals. Distinguish motion removal from emotion removal; instructions define artefact conditions, not an exhaustive artefact ground truth. The present P001 sample supports a quality-pipeline prototype, not validation of emotion prediction or population transfer.

### 3. Does resting-state normalization reduce cohort shift without erasing individual affect?

Hypothesis: normalization to each participant's quiet resting baseline improves cross-cohort EEG-autonomic rating prediction, especially when rating tools change, while preserving within-person response variation.

Required fields: participant/cohort/day identity, age/gender for coverage checks, rating values and collection provenance, subjective emotion, video/order, flags; resting-state and emotion-video EEG/ECG/GSR/respiration; event timing and maps. Confirm baseline availability per session and quantify cohort missingness before selecting cohorts.

Experiment: leave one cohort out, comparing global training-only scaling, within-session resting subtraction/ratios and late fusion with modality dropout. Also hold videos out to assess stimulus shortcuts. Use only pre-video baseline samples from the evaluation session, with this calibration access declared; compare with a no-test-baseline setting. Evaluate per-cohort MAE, participant-level calibration and performance versus missing-modality availability. Match trial counts and stratify flag inclusion. A gain from cohort identification alone would not support robust emotional decoding.

**Strongest next question: question 1**, because it directly tests the dataset's individualized stimulus design and the principal stimulus-recognition confound. First run the CSV audit to measure expected/default disagreement, subjective-label coverage and video overlap; this is a CPU-only feasibility gate before any modeling. Question 2 is the best immediate use of the existing P001 signals while training labels remain unavailable. Originality here means a concrete proposed angle, not a verified novelty claim from a literature review.

## Reproduction and outputs

From the repository root, install `scripts/eda/requirements.txt` into a Python 3.12 environment, then run:

```powershell
python scripts/eda/run.py --data data --sample sample --out reports/eda
```

No RNG, GPU, network call or model fit is used. `--sample` must point to a deliberately small recording subset; do not point it to the entire dataset. Native pyEDFlib reads avoid the example loader's whole-recording DataFrame and eye-stream reconstruction; the supplied GSR calibration function is reused. Optional `pypdf` was installed locally for reading the paper and is not a runtime EDA dependency. `manifest.json` records exact library versions, input sizes and SHA-256 digests. CSVs retain field-level metrics for review. Signal traces and EEG PSD PNGs are produced for each supplied processing version/task. Future CSV outputs include cohort/participant/session counts, missingness by cohort, emotion distributions, rating validation and histograms, annotation-source agreement/confusions and combined outlier counts. Missing table analyses are reported as unavailable, never fabricated or replaced with sample metadata.
